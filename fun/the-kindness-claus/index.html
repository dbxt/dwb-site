The Kindness Claus

How a white beard, a panicked phone call, and a next-day Amazon delivery turned into five years of dancing with a million lights.

By David Wayne Baxter · September 21, 2026 · 2 min read

Thanks to genetics, my beard turned almost completely white in my early 30s. I did not ask for it, and I had no idea yet that it was a qualification.

The call that started it.

When my son was in preschool, the school’s regular Santa was unable to visit because of a prolonged illness. The school administrator called me in a panic. By any chance, did I have a Santa suit? Or would I be willing to buy one, so the kids could still get a visit?

Not wanting the children to be left without, I did what any calm and resourceful adult would do. I opened Amazon Prime, found the best suit I could, and paid for next-day delivery.

The suit arrived. More than 120 preschoolers were waiting. Their reactions ran from overwhelming excitement to tearful dread, and a preschool Santa learns to love both. We made Christmas a success.

Not wanting the children to be left without, I ordered the best suit I could find.

Then came the lights.

A few years later, I was asked to play Santa again. This time it was for a neighborhood light show. I was skeptical, right up until I spoke with the owners.

They have more than a million lights, synchronized to an entire program. Over the years they have added a circus theme, with aerial acrobats, firebreathers, dancers, and a bevy of neighborhood children, from toy soldiers to elves. This is not a yard display. It is a production.

From waving to a fight scene.

My part began as the classic: stand there and wave. Then Santa evolved. There are now full dance numbers, and there is a fight scene with the Grinch.

If you had told me in that preschool that Santa would one day be in a fight scene, I would have asked whether the suit came with insurance.

I spend my working life leading engineering teams, so I have real respect for a production like this. Getting that many lights, performers, and children to hit the right beat at the right time is a coordination problem, and this crew solves it with a smile.

Why I keep coming back.

I have been part of this news-featured performance for five years, and I look forward to it more every year.

It began with not wanting a room full of preschoolers to go without a Santa. Everything since has been the same job, with better lighting.
