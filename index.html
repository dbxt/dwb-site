How I lead

Five working principles.

Learn the system before changing it.

I have a record of joining active projects and teams, understanding how they actually work, and improving efficiency and quality of output without interrupting delivery.

Turn priorities into work engineers can act on.

I work with product managers and stakeholders to define scope and requirements and to set expectations early, so that developers receive clear tasks and everyone knows what will be delivered.

Make the safe path the easy path.

Reliability and release confidence are engineering products in their own right. I invest in observability, automated validation, and rollback automation so that teams can ship often without depending on individual heroics.

Hire for the team, not just the résumé.

I weigh compatibility, personality, and drive alongside technical ability when I hire. Teams built that way work well together, and they excel together.

Invest in the people doing the work.

I learn each engineer’s goals and build career paths with clear milestones. Tech talks, innovation projects, and short-term goals keep skills growing, and the result has been dedicated teams with near-flawless retention, including through changes of priority. I also mentor engineers outside my own teams.
